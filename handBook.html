DigiLaya is not about becoming a computer expert.

It's about becoming more capable of managing your own digital life. The program is a community-based effort to help residents build practical digital skills for everyday use — checking a device's security, organizing important files, telling a real message from a scam, and knowing what to do if something goes wrong.

Digital self-reliance doesn't mean solving every problem alone. It means you know what you can do yourself, what you should avoid, and when and how to ask for safe help.

Before we begin: your safety comes first.

Facilitators will guide you through activities, but you stay in control of your own device and accounts. A facilitator should never ask you for any of this:

  • Passwords
  • One-Time Passwords (OTPs)
  • PINs
  • Recovery codes
  • Banking information
  • Full payment-card details
  • Secret answers
  • Authentication codes

You may ask for help with a digital task, but keep your confidential information private.

Using your own device during the session

  1. Use your own smartphone whenever possible.
  2. Read instructions before tapping.
  3. Ask questions when you are unsure.
  4. Do not install unknown applications just because someone tells you to.
  5. Do not click suspicious links.
  6. Do not send passwords or OTPs to anyone.
  7. Stop immediately if something feels suspicious.

02

Secure

Your accounts and devices hold valuable information. Security starts with simple habits.

1. Lock your device

Use a PIN, password, pattern, fingerprint, or face authentication. Avoid easy-to-guess combinations like 1234, 0000, your birthday, your name, or other publicly known information.

2. Use strong, unique passwords

Avoid reusing the same password everywhere — the same one for Facebook, email, shopping, and banking is a risky habit. Different passwords for important accounts is safer. A reputable password manager can help.

3. Turn on additional security

Where available, enable multi-factor authentication — authentication apps, security keys, or device confirmation — for your important accounts.

4. Keep your device updated

Updates bring security fixes, bug fixes, and performance improvements. Review and install legitimate updates when your phone or apps notify you.

Organize

Important information gets hard to find once a phone fills up with photos, downloads, screenshots, and documents. Good organization saves time and reduces stress.

04

Verify

Not everything you see online is true. Scammers and misinformation use urgency, fake promotions, fake government notices, fake delivery updates, and fake job offers to make you react before you check.

Stop → Check → Verify → Act

Stop

Don't immediately click, reply, transfer money, or share information. Ask yourself: "Why am I receiving this?"

Check

Look carefully at the sender, phone number, email address, website address, spelling, message details, and what's being requested.

Verify

Confirm using a trusted source — open the organization's official app or website yourself instead of clicking the link in the message.

Act

Only proceed once you're reasonably confident it's legitimate. Still unsure? Don't proceed — ask a trusted person or contact the organization through an official channel.

Activity 4 — Real or suspicious?

"CONGRATULATIONS! You have won ₱50,000! Claim your prize now by clicking this link and providing your account information."

Correct approach: stop and verify — never click the link or send account information immediately.

05

Prepare

Losing account access can happen — a forgotten password, a lost or damaged phone, an inaccessible email, a compromised account, or outdated recovery details. Preparing ahead makes recovery easier.

For your important accounts, check whether your recovery information is current, accessible, correct, and controlled by you. Depending on the service, that might mean a recovery email, recovery phone number, trusted devices, authentication methods, or backup codes.

Never share recovery codes with other people. Store them securely, following the service's own instructions.

Digital emergency checklist.

If you think your account or device has been compromised, work through this in order.

Stop

Don't continue interacting with suspicious messages.

Secure

Change affected passwords using a trusted device or official service.

Check

Review recent account activity and security notifications.

Recover

Use the service's official account-recovery process.

Report

If appropriate, report suspicious activity to the relevant platform, organization, or authorities.

Ask for safe help

Ask someone you trust for assistance.

Never give your password, OTP, PIN, or recovery code to someone who claims they need it to "help" you.

Quick reference guide.

When you receive a suspicious message

Stop → don't click immediately → check who sent it → verify with an official source → act only when confident

When you forget your password

  1. Go to the official application or website.
  2. Select the official password-recovery option.
  3. Follow the service's instructions.
  4. Don't give your recovery code to another person.
  5. Create a new, strong password.
  6. Review your account security afterward.

When your phone is lost

  1. Stay calm.
  2. Use the device-finding service available on your device.
  3. Secure important accounts.
  4. Contact your mobile provider when necessary.
  5. Change important passwords if needed.
  6. Ask for help from a trusted person or appropriate service.

Becoming a DigiLaya Community Guide.

A Community Guide is a trained community volunteer who helps share practical digital self-reliance practices with other residents. They don't need to know everything about technology — they need to be patient, respectful, responsible, willing to learn, careful with participants' privacy, and able to explain simple digital tasks clearly.

Before

Prepare materials, confirm participants, review activity instructions, check equipment, and coordinate with the implementation team.

During

Guide participants through activities, answer basic questions, help with navigation, observe understanding, and protect participant privacy.

After

Record concerns, encourage participants to follow their 30-day plans, help organize replication sessions, and give feedback to the project team.

A Community Guide must never

  • Ask for a participant's password
  • Ask for an OTP
  • Ask for a recovery code
  • Log into a participant's account for personal purposes
  • Copy private files without permission
  • Access private messages without permission
  • Request financial information unnecessarily
  • Use a participant's account for them without a valid reason

Guide the person. Don't take control of the person's digital life.
